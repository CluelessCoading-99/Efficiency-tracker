Excel & CSV Layout Guide

How Your Excel File Must Look

Keep your Excel sheet simple with just these 5 columns in order (Columns A to E). You do not need descriptions, extra info, or comments.

A: Technician B: WIP C: Date D: Requested_Hrs E: Given_Hrs
Richard Harrison 48291 2026-11-23 2.5 2.5
Richard Harrison 48304 2026-11-23 1.8 1.5
Richard Harrison 48319 2026-11-23 3.0
1. Column A (Technician): Must match the technician selected in Settings (Richard Harrison). Rows for other technicians are ignored.
2. Column B (WIP): Your job WIP number. If the same WIP appears twice in the file, its hours are automatically added together.
3. Column C (Date): Use YYYY-MM-DD format (for example, 2026-11-23).
4. Columns D & E (Hours): Put called hours in D and invoiced/given hours in E (leave E blank if still waiting).
5. Save as CSV: In Excel, click File → Save As → CSV (Comma delimited) (*.csv), then upload that file here.
Data Safety Guide

How JSON Backup & Restore Works

Your jobs are stored inside this computer's web browser. Backing up creates a small safety file on your computer in case your browser cache is ever cleared or you move to a new PC.

1. How to Back Up (Every Tuesday & Friday):
Click Backup JSON. Your browser will download a file named workshop_backup_YYYY-MM-DD.json into your Downloads folder (or save it to your personal folder/USB stick).
2. How to Restore If Data Goes Missing:
Open Settings, tap Choose File under Restore JSON Database Backup, pick your latest .json backup file, and click Restore JSON. All your jobs, targets, and years will come right back.
3. Do Not Edit the JSON File:
You don't need to open or change the .json file in Excel—just keep the newest copy safe.
